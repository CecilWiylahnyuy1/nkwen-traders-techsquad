Why we exist

A shop that started because a child came home empty-handed

In 1994, a woman named Beatrice Ngwa sent her son to a neighborhood shop in Nkwen for semovita and palm oil. He came back with neither — the shop had run out of both, again. It wasn't the first time. Mama Beatrice, as she'd later be known to half of Bamenda, decided that if no one else was going to fix that problem, she would.

She opened a single shopfront with one rule guiding every decision: carry what a household actually runs out of, and never let the shelf go empty. No frills, no guesswork — just semovita, palm oil, cocoyam flour, and groundnuts, restocked like clockwork.

"If a mother sends her child for semovita and palm oil, that child should not come home empty-handed. That is the whole business." — Mama Beatrice Ngwa, Founder

Why it grew beyond one shop

Word travels fast when a shop actually keeps its promises. Within a decade, neighbors from other parts of Bamenda were asking Mama Beatrice to open closer to them. Each new branch kept the same rule from day one — the same core staples, always in stock — while slowly adding what each neighborhood asked for: a weekly chin-chin bake in Kondengui, a honey partnership near Bafut, imported goods for the back-to-school rush.

Thirty years later, that's still the whole philosophy. Nkwen Traders didn't grow by chasing trends — it grew because it kept answering the same simple question a little better each year: what does this neighborhood keep running out of, and how do we make sure they never have to ask twice?

How we got here

  • 1994 Nkwen Main branch opens in Bamenda with a single storeroom of pantry staples — the shop that started it all.
  • 2003 The Kondengui branch opens, and the weekly chin-chin bake becomes a neighborhood fixture people plan their Fridays around.
  • 2011 Nkwen Traders partners directly with honey cooperatives near Bafut, opening its first specialty aisle.
  • 2019 A sixth branch opens, and Nkwen Traders begins sourcing Bamenda highland coffee in small batches.
  • 2026 Nkwen Traders goes online for the first time — this website is that next chapter.

Why we're online now

For thirty years, the only way to know what Nkwen Traders had in stock was to walk in and look. This website exists to change that one thing, and nothing else — you can check the catalog, learn which branch is closest, and reach out before you ever leave the house. The shelves haven't changed. Now you just don't have to guess what's on them.
